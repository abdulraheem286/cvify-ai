Resume action verbs are strong doing words that start a bullet point and name what you actually did, such as *led*, *built*, *cut*, *negotiated*, or *rebuilt*. They matter because the first word of every bullet is the word a recruiter is most likely to read, and a verb turns a flat job description into evidence of what you contributed.

Most CVs are not weak because the person lacked experience. They are weak because good work is buried under phrases like "responsible for the management of" and "assisted with various tasks". Swapping in a precise verb is the fastest edit you can make to a CV, and it costs you nothing but a few minutes.

## How to use an action verb properly

A verb on its own is not enough. The strongest bullets follow a simple shape:

**Action verb + what you did + the result.**

So instead of "managed social media", you get "grew the company's Instagram following from 2,000 to 11,000 in eight months". The verb opens the door, and the result closes the sale.

Three rules keep this working:

- **One verb per bullet, at the front.** Do not bury it after a phrase like "In my role, I…".
- **Match the verb to the size of the work.** *Led* means you were in charge. *Supported* means you were not. Overstating is the fastest way to come unstuck in an interview.
- **Vary your verbs.** If eight bullets in a row start with *managed*, the reader stops noticing any of them.

Use present tense for the job you are in now and past tense for jobs you have left. If you want the full structure that sits around these bullets, see [how to list work experience](/blog/how-to-list-work-experience-on-cv).

## Before and after: three rewrites

Seeing the edit is more useful than reading about it. In each pair below, the facts are the same. Only the wording changed.

> **Before:** Responsible for handling customer complaints and queries.
>
> **After:** Resolved up to 60 customer complaints a week and lifted the team's satisfaction score from 78% to 91% over six months.

> **Before:** Duties included updating the company website and social media accounts.
>
> **After:** Rebuilt the company website and published weekly content across three social channels, doubling monthly enquiries from the contact form.

> **Before:** Helped with the onboarding process for new starters.
>
> **After:** Redesigned the new-starter onboarding pack and trained 14 new hires, cutting their time to first solo shift from three weeks to nine days.

Notice that the "after" versions are barely longer. You are not padding the bullet, you are replacing empty words with real ones.

## Overused words and phrases to cut

Some phrases turn up on almost every CV, which is exactly why they no longer signal anything. Cut these:

- **"Responsible for"** — it describes the job title you already listed, not what you did with it. Delete it and promote the verb that follows. "Responsible for managing stock" becomes "managed stock".
- **"Duties included"** — the same problem, plus it frames your work as a list of chores rather than achievements.
- **"Tasked with"** and **"helped with"** — both suggest someone else drove the work. If you did the thing, say so plainly.
- **"Team player", "hard worker", "go-getter", "results-driven"** — these are claims about your personality that the reader cannot check. A bullet showing you trained three colleagues proves you are a team player far better than the phrase ever will.
- **"Various"** and **"a range of"** — vague filler. Name the actual thing you worked on.

The test is simple. If a candidate with none of your experience could write the same sentence, it is not doing any work for you.

## Key takeaways

- Start every bullet point with a strong action verb, then say what you did and what changed as a result.
- Rotate your verbs across categories — leadership, results, improvement, creation, analysis, communication, and support — so no single word repeats too often.
- Match the verb to your real level of involvement. *Led* and *supported* are not interchangeable.
- Cut "responsible for", "duties included", and personality claims like "team player"; they take up space and prove nothing.
- Present tense for your current job, past tense for everything else, and never mix the two inside one bullet.
