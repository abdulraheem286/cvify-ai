To prepare for a job interview, research the company and role, re-read the job description and your CV, practice your answers to common questions, prepare a few questions of your own, and plan the logistics like time, place, and outfit. Good preparation is what turns nervous guessing into calm, clear answers.

Most people walk into interviews hoping to "wing it." The candidates who stand out do the opposite: they treat preparation as a short, simple checklist. Below is that checklist, step by step, so you know exactly what to do before the big day.

## Start with a simple preparation checklist

Here is the whole plan in one place. Work through it in the days before your interview:

- **Research the company and the role.** Know what they do and why the job exists.
- **Re-read the job description and your CV.** Match your experience to their needs.
- **Practice common answers out loud.** Use the STAR method for story-based questions.
- **Prepare your own questions.** Have three or four ready to ask them.
- **Plan the logistics.** Sort out the time, place, travel, and outfit.
- **Prepare for virtual interviews.** Test your tech if the interview is online.

Each step below explains how to do it well. You do not need hours. Even an evening of focused prep makes a real difference.

## Step 1: Research the company and the role

Start by learning what the company actually does. Read its website, especially the "About" and "Products" or "Services" pages. Look for recent news, a blog post, or a product launch you can mention.

Then go a level deeper:

- Who are their customers, and what problem do they solve?
- What is their tone, formal or relaxed? This hints at the culture.
- Who might interview you? A quick LinkedIn search on the hiring manager helps.

You are not trying to memorise everything. You just want enough to speak about them as if you already care about the work, because a little genuine interest is easy to spot.

## Step 2: Re-read the job description and your CV

The job description is your cheat sheet. It tells you exactly which skills the employer values most. Read it slowly and highlight the key duties and requirements.

Now put your CV next to it. For each main requirement, find a matching example from your own experience. If the job asks for "team leadership," think of a specific time you led a team and what happened.

This step does two things. It reminds you of your own best stories, and it makes sure your answers connect directly to what they need. If you want your CV to match a role even more closely before you apply, you can [build your CV free](/build) and tailor it to the job.

## Step 3: Practice answers to common questions

You cannot predict every question, but you can prepare for the usual ones. Think through how you would answer "Tell me about yourself," "Why do you want this job," and "What is your biggest weakness."

For questions about past experience, use the **STAR method**. STAR is a simple way to tell a clear story:

- **Situation** — set the scene in one line.
- **Task** — what you needed to do.
- **Action** — the steps you actually took.
- **Result** — what happened, ideally with a number or clear outcome.

STAR keeps your answers focused instead of rambling. Practice out loud, not just in your head, because speaking a story is very different from thinking it. For a full list to rehearse with, see these [common interview questions and answers](/blog/common-interview-questions-and-answers).

## Step 4: Prepare your own questions to ask

Near the end, almost every interviewer asks, "Do you have any questions for us?" Saying "no" is a missed chance. Good questions show you are serious and thinking ahead.

Prepare three or four questions, so you still have some left if a few get answered during the chat. Strong options include:

- What does success look like in this role in the first six months?
- What are the biggest challenges the team is facing right now?
- How would you describe the team culture?
- What are the next steps in the hiring process?

Avoid asking only about salary and holidays in a first interview. Focus on the work and the team; there is time for the details later.

## Step 5: Plan the logistics and your outfit

Small practical details can throw you off if you leave them to the last minute. Sort them out the day before so your mind is free on interview day.

Run through this quick list:

- **Confirm the time and format.** In person, phone, or video call?
- **Check the location or link.** Know the address, or test the meeting link.
- **Plan your travel.** Aim to arrive about ten minutes early, not thirty, not late.
- **Choose your outfit.** Dress one step smarter than the company's daily style, and lay your clothes out the night before.
- **Pack your bag.** Printed CVs, a notepad, a pen, your questions, and any ID or documents they asked for.

Getting these right means you arrive calm and on time, instead of stressed and rushing.

## Step 6: Prepare for a virtual interview

Online interviews are now normal, and they have their own small traps. A few minutes of setup prevents most of them.

Before the call:

- **Test your tech.** Check your camera, microphone, and internet connection.
- **Pick your spot.** Sit somewhere quiet and well lit, with a tidy background.
- **Set up your view.** Put the camera at eye level and look into it when you speak, so it feels like eye contact.
- **Reduce distractions.** Close other apps, silence your phone, and tell people at home not to interrupt.
- **Have notes ready.** Keep your CV and question list open or nearby, where you can glance without staring away.

Join a couple of minutes early. It shows you are keen and gives you a moment to settle before you begin.

## The night before and the day of

The evening before, do a light review, then stop. Read your notes once, check your bag or your tech, and go to bed at a normal time. Cramming late rarely helps and often hurts.

On the day, eat something, arrive or log in early, and take a slow breath before you start. Remember that an interview is a two-way conversation. They want to see if you fit, and you are checking if they fit you too. That mindset alone takes the pressure down.

## Key takeaways

- **Preparation beats improvisation.** Research the company, re-read the job description, and match your CV to what they need.
- **Practice out loud.** Rehearse common questions and use the STAR method to keep story answers clear and focused.
- **Bring your own questions.** Three or four thoughtful questions show real interest and help you decide if the job fits you.
- **Sort the logistics early.** Confirm the time, place, outfit, and documents the day before so nothing surprises you.
- **Treat virtual interviews seriously.** Test your tech, tidy your space, and look into the camera to make a strong impression.
