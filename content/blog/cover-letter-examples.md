A strong cover letter is a one-page note that names the exact job, proves in one or two short paragraphs that you can do it, and ends with a clear next step. The best way to learn the format fast is to read a few cover letter examples, then copy their shape (not their words) with your own details. Below are short examples for different situations, plus a simple structure you can reuse for any job.

Use these cover letter examples as a starting shape, not a script. Read the one closest to your situation, notice how it names the role, gives proof, and closes, then rewrite it in your own voice with your own achievements.

## What every good cover letter has in common

Before the examples, here is the structure they all follow. A cover letter has four parts:

- **Header** — your name, phone, professional email, and city (skip most of this in an email).
- **Greeting and opening** — address a real person if you can, then name the exact role and one reason you fit.
- **Body** — one or two short paragraphs that prove two or three things the job needs, with real examples and numbers.
- **Closing** — a short, warm sign-off with a clear call to action, usually asking for a conversation.

Keep the whole thing to one page, usually 250 to 400 words. For a full walkthrough of each part, see our guide on [how to write a cover letter](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter). Now to the examples.

## Cover letter example: experienced professional

Use this when you have several years in your field and clear results to point to. Lead with a number.

> Dear Ms. Okafor,
>
> I'm applying for the Operations Manager role at Northwind Logistics. Over the past six years I've run warehouse teams of up to 40 people and cut order errors by 22% while keeping despatch on schedule during our busiest peak.
>
> Your job post mentions scaling fulfilment without adding headcount. That's exactly the problem I solved last year: I redesigned our pick-and-pack flow and lifted throughput by roughly 30% with the same team. I also trained two supervisors who now run their own shifts.
>
> I'd welcome the chance to show how I can help Northwind grow without breaking your service levels. Thank you for reading, and I'm happy to talk whenever suits you.
>
> Best regards,
> Daniel Osei

Notice the pattern: role, a headline result, a requirement from the post matched to proof, then a warm close. That is the core of most strong cover letter examples.

## Cover letter example: career changer

Use this when you are moving into a new field. Name the switch openly, then show that your old skills transfer.

> Dear Hiring Team,
>
> I'm applying for the Junior UX Designer role at Loomly. I'm moving into design from five years in customer support, where I spent every day learning why people get stuck and how to make things simpler for them.
>
> That work taught me the heart of good UX: listen first, then remove friction. Over the last year I completed a UX design course, rebuilt our help centre (which cut repeat tickets by 18%), and designed two app prototypes you can see in my portfolio. I know I'm early in design, but I bring real user empathy and I learn fast.
>
> I'd love to bring that outside-in view to your team. Thank you for considering me, and I'd welcome a quick call.
>
> Kind regards,
> Priya Sharma

A career-change letter does more work than a normal one, so the body is where you connect the dots. If you have an employment gap to explain, our guide on [how to write a cover letter](/blog/how-to-write-a-cover-letter) covers that too.

## Cover letter example: recent graduate

Use this when you have little paid experience. Lean on your degree, projects, internships, and part-time work.

> Dear Mr. Bennett,
>
> I'm applying for the Marketing Assistant role at Brightseed. I recently graduated with a degree in Marketing, and I'm keen to start my career with a brand known for creative, honest campaigns like yours.
>
> During my studies I ran the social accounts for our student union and grew the Instagram following from 400 to 2,600 in one term by testing content weekly and posting what worked. My final project, a go-to-market plan for a local café, earned a distinction. I'm organised, curious, and comfortable with tools like Canva and Google Analytics.
>
> I'd love the chance to bring that energy to Brightseed. Thank you for your time, and I'd welcome an interview.
>
> Best wishes,
> Leah Nguyen

No experience yet? Real proof still exists in coursework, clubs, and side projects. Our guide on [how to write a CV with no experience](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-with-no-experience) shows how to find it.

## Cover letter example: short and simple

Use this for a quick online application, an email, or when the form only allows a small box. Say who you are, why you fit, and what you want, in a few lines.

> Dear Hiring Team,
>
> I'm applying for the Bookkeeper role at Vale Accounting. I've managed the books for two small businesses for the past three years, handling invoicing, payroll, and monthly reconciliations with zero missed deadlines. I'm certified in Xero and QuickBooks and I'm known for spotting errors before they cost anything.
>
> I'd welcome a conversation about how I can help keep Vale's accounts accurate and on time. Thank you for reading.
>
> Kind regards,
> Marcus Reid

Short does not mean weak. Even a four-sentence letter can name the role, give one solid proof point, and ask for the next step.

## How to turn an example into your own letter

The examples above share the same skeleton, so borrow the skeleton and fill it with your facts:

- **Swap the role and company** in the opening line, every single time.
- **Pick two or three requirements** from the job post and match each to a real example.
- **Add a number** wherever you honestly can (a percentage, a time saved, a count).
- **Rewrite in your own voice** so it sounds like you, not a sample.
- **Trim to one page** and read it aloud to catch clumsy lines.

Most of this raw material already lives in your CV. The achievements and numbers you gathered there feed straight into your cover letter, which is why it helps to [build your CV free](/build) first, then pull your best two or three points into the letter.

The main thing to avoid is sending the exact same letter everywhere. Recruiters and ATS (applicant tracking systems, the software that scans applications) both reward specifics, and a generic letter stands out for the wrong reason.

## Key takeaways

- A strong cover letter is one page: header, opening, body, and closing.
- Read cover letter examples for their shape, then rewrite them with your own details.
- Match your opening and body to two or three things the specific job needs.
- Add real numbers and proof; adjectives alone do not convince anyone.
- Never send one generic letter to every job, even if you only change a little.

The fastest way to use these cover letter examples is to pick the one closest to your situation, keep its structure, and replace every line with your own story. Get the role, the person, and the proof right, and the formatting takes care of itself.
