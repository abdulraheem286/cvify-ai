Yes — send a short, specific thank you email after an interview, ideally within 24 hours. It takes five minutes, and it is one of the few moments after an interview where you still have some control over the impression you leave.

Most candidates either skip it or send something so generic it reads like a form letter. A note that quotes one real moment from your conversation stands out immediately. Here is how to write one, plus three templates you can adapt.

## Why a thank you email actually helps

It works for three simple reasons.

First, it keeps your name in front of the interviewer while they are still deciding. Hiring teams often interview several people over a few days, and the conversations blur together. A short note that references something specific pulls your face back into focus.

Second, it shows manners and follow-through. Interviewers read your behaviour after the interview as a preview of your behaviour as a colleague.

Third, it gives you a second shot. If you fumbled a question, or forgot to mention a relevant project, the thank you note is your one clean chance to fix it without looking desperate.

## When to send a thank you email after an interview

Same day is best. Within 24 hours is the rule.

The reason is simple: the interviewer's memory of you is sharpest in the first day, and hiring decisions sometimes move faster than you expect. If your interview finished at 6pm, sending it first thing the next morning is completely fine and often lands better than a note fired off at midnight.

If several days have already passed, still send it. A late thank you is better than none, just keep it shorter and skip any apology for the delay.

## What subject line should I use?

Keep it plain and easy to recognise in a full inbox. Any of these work:

- Thank you — [Job title] interview
- Thanks for your time today
- Great speaking with you about the [Job title] role

Avoid clever or vague lines. The interviewer should know who you are and why you are writing before they open it. If you are replying to an existing thread with the recruiter, just reply there — the thread already carries the context.

## What to include

A good note has five short parts, in this order:

- **A thank you.** One line. Name the role and the day.
- **One specific reference.** Something they said, a project they described, a challenge the team is facing, or a question that made you think.
- **A line on fit.** Connect that specific thing to something you have actually done. One sentence, no CV recap.
- **Optional repair line.** A better answer to a question you handled badly, in one or two sentences.
- **A close.** Repeat your interest and say you are happy to share anything else they need.

That is roughly 100 to 150 words. Anything longer starts to feel like a second application, and the interviewer will skim it.

## What to reference from the conversation

The specific detail is the whole point of the email, so pick it carefully. Good options include a project or product they mentioned, a problem they said the team is trying to solve, a change in how the team works, or a moment where you and the interviewer genuinely agreed on something.

Write it down straight after the interview, before you forget. Two or three notes on your phone is enough — see [how to prepare for a job interview](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-a-job-interview) for what to get ready beforehand.

Avoid referencing anything personal that they shared in passing. Mentioning their holiday plans or their kids feels like you were collecting material rather than listening.

## How to fix an answer you got wrong

If a question caught you out, address it in one or two sentences and move on. Do not re-litigate the whole interview.

Something like: "You asked how I would handle a project with no clear owner. I gave a vague answer, so here is a clearer one — on my last team I..." Then give the short version.

Two rules here. Keep it brief, because a long correction draws more attention to the weak answer than the answer itself did. And only do this once, for the single worst moment, not for every answer you wish you had phrased better.

## Three templates you can copy

Change the details. If you send any of these unedited, it will read exactly like what it is.

**Standard interview**

> Subject: Thank you — Marketing Executive interview
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> Thank you for your time this morning. I really enjoyed hearing how the team is rebuilding the email programme from scratch this quarter.
>
> That part stuck with me because I ran a similar rebuild at my last role, and the segmentation work you described is exactly the kind of problem I like getting into.
>
> I am very interested in the role, and happy to share anything else that would help. Thanks again.
>
> Best,
> Ahmed

**After a panel interview**

> Subject: Thanks for your time today
>
> Hi Daniel,
>
> Thank you for meeting me today, and please pass my thanks to Priya and Tom as well.
>
> I appreciated how honest you were about the pressure the support team is under during launches. It is the kind of challenge I have handled before, and I would be glad to help build a process that holds up under that load.
>
> I left the conversation more interested in the role than when I walked in. Do let me know if you need anything further from me.
>
> Best,
> Ahmed

**After a final-round interview**

> Subject: Thank you — final interview for Operations Manager
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> Thank you for the conversation yesterday, and for the time the whole team has given me across the process.
>
> Meeting the wider team made the role much more concrete for me. Your point about wanting someone who fixes the reporting mess rather than just documenting it is exactly the work I want to be doing.
>
> I would be delighted to join, and I am happy to answer anything else as you finalise your decision.
>
> Best,
> Ahmed

## What not to do

A few things quietly work against you.

**Do not send an obvious copy-paste.** If the note could be sent to any company, it adds nothing. Interviewers spot the template shape instantly.

**Do not chase.** The thank you email is not a follow-up. Send it once, then wait for the timeline they gave you. If that date passes with no word, that is when a separate, polite check-in makes sense — see [how to follow up on a job application](/blog/how-to-follow-up-on-a-job-application) for how to word it.

**Do not get over-familiar.** Friendly is good, matey is not. Skip jokes, emojis, and lines like "can't wait to join the team" before an offer exists.

**Do not attach your CV again.** They have it. Adding it looks like you think they lost it.

**Do not ask for feedback in the same email.** Mixing gratitude with a request muddies the message. Ask for feedback later, if you get a rejection.

## Key takeaways

- Send a thank you email after an interview within 24 hours, same day if you can.
- Keep it to 100 to 150 words with a plain, recognisable subject line.
- Reference one specific thing from the conversation — this is what separates it from every other note in the inbox.
- Use one or two sentences to fix a weak answer, then stop.
- Send a separate, slightly different note to each interviewer, and do not chase after it.
